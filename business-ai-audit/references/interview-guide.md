# Targeted follow-ups

Use when a vague answer, unclear bottleneck, or competing tasks prevents progress. Select one follow-up from the missing evidence, not a tour of business functions.

| Signal | Investigate | Possible next question |
|---|---|---|
| “Everything takes too long” | A manageable starting point | Which task interrupted your work most often last week? |
| Copying between tools | Source, destination, fixed rules | Show me the last thing you copied and where it went. |
| Searching for information | Authoritative source and needed answer | What were you trying to find, and where did you look? |
| Repeated replies | Approved information and exceptions | What did the last customer ask, and what did you need to check? |
| Waiting or follow-ups | Dependency versus handling time | What exactly were you waiting for before you could continue? |
| Inconsistent decisions | Good result and difficult case | What made the last difficult case different from the usual one? |
| Lack of capacity | Useful work currently omitted | What important activity keeps getting postponed? |
| Multiple symptoms | Shared cause | Did those problems start from the same missing information? |

An owner may describe a symptom before the task. Offer two plausible interpretations grounded in their words, label them as possibilities, and let the owner correct them. Supply a proposed default for a decision only when evidence supports it; the owner's facts cannot be supplied as suggested answers.

Introduce constraints at the decision they affect: acceptable maintenance before proposing custom infrastructure; budget before a paid service; human boundaries before external actions. Resolve the business timezone before scheduling rather than assuming it from the computer.

Example of the reasoning, not a prescribed workflow: a consultant loses time preparing client handoffs. Trace the last handoff before proposing a chatbot. The cause might be missing intake information; a standard form could be enough. If gathering scattered evidence is the problem, a sourced draft brief may help. Ask what changed the handoff outcome before choosing either.
